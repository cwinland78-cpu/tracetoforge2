TracetoForge vs Tooltrace vs GridPilot vs Tracefinity (2026)

Published 2026-05-07 · Updated 2026-10-15 by Chris Winland

Quick Answer

All four turn a photo of a tool on paper into a Gridfinity bin. Pick TracetoForge for pay-per-export pricing with no subscription, up to 12 tools per insert, and Packout or ToughSystem tray templates. Pick Tooltrace if you also cut foam shadow boxes or need STEP files. Pick GridPilot for pricing by area. Pick Tracefinity if you want magnet holes and labels, or want to self-host open source.

Disclosure: I built TracetoForge, so read this with that in mind. Every fact about the other tools below comes from their own websites, linked in the sources section, and was checked on 15 October 2026. Where a site does not say something, the table says "not listed" rather than guessing. Pricing in this category changes often, so confirm before you pay.

The Four Tools at a Glance

  • TracetoForge: browser-based editor. Tracing runs in your browser with OpenCV.js. Modes for Gridfinity bins (with optional stacking lip), free-form trays, gaskets, and inserts sized for Milwaukee PACKOUT and DeWalt ToughSystem 2.0 organizers. Up to 12 tools per insert. Exports STL, 3MF, SVG and DXF. Tracing and preview are free; exports cost credits ($9.99 for 20, $34.99 for 100) that never expire.
  • Tooltrace: made by finalREV, a machine shop in Berkeley. Turns a photo into Gridfinity inserts or foam shadow boxes. Multiple tools per layout with per-tool pocket depth. Exports STL, 3MF, STEP, DXF and SVG, plus a 1:1 PDF for checking size. Free for 3 active traces; Pro is $8/month.
  • GridPilot: AI tool detection from a photo, plus manual tools, zones and auto-arrange. Gridfinity or custom dimensions. Exports DXF and 3MF. Priced by area: 100 square inches a month free, $3.99 for 4,500 square inches, or $16.99/month unlimited.
  • Tracefinity: MIT-licensed open source, available hosted or self-hosted in Docker. Bin builder with a tool library, magnet holes, stacking lip, finger holes and labels. Exports STL, 3MF (for bins with labels) and SVG outlines. Hosted plans: free (2 bins), €4.99/month or €9.99/month. Covered by Hackaday.

When to Pick TracetoForge

  • You don't want a subscription. Tracing and 3D preview are free without an account. Exports use credits that never expire, so occasional users pay only for what they export.
  • You're filling a Packout or ToughSystem organizer. Built-in templates size the insert for the PACKOUT Compact (48-22-8435), Low-Profile (48-22-8436), SHOCKWAVE Customizable Case (48-32-9921) and ToughSystem 2.0 (DWST08165).
  • You're laying out a whole drawer. Up to 12 tools per insert, each with its own depth, tolerance and finger notch.
  • You'd rather your photos weren't uploaded. All image processing happens in your browser using OpenCV.js. If you save a project to your account, a thumbnail is stored.

Where it falls short: no magnet holes and no labels yet. If you need those in the file, Tracefinity or Tooltrace Pro covers them.

When to Pick Tooltrace

Tooltrace is the broadest on file formats, including STEP for CAD work, and it does both Gridfinity inserts and foam shadow boxes from the same trace. If your shop uses foam drawers as well as printed bins, it handles both. Note that some Pro features can be used while designing but need an upgrade before you export.

When to Pick GridPilot

GridPilot prices by area rather than by export or by month, and adds zones and auto-arrange for laying out several tools. If you mostly make small inserts, the 100 square inch free allowance may cover you.

When to Pick Tracefinity

Tracefinity has the most Gridfinity-specific options of the four (magnet holes, labels, stacking lip, splitting bins that are too big for your printer bed), and it's open source. If you're comfortable running Docker, self-hosting is free with no limits, and your photos stay on your own machine. The hosted free tier is small, at 2 bins.

What They All Have in Common

Each one uses a sheet of paper in the photo for scale, so photo quality matters more than the tool. Shoot straight down, light evenly, and use a plain background. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Tracefinity and TracetoForge?

Tracefinity is MIT-licensed open source. You can self-host it in Docker or use the hosted app at tracefinity.net, where photos are processed on its servers. It exports STL, 3MF (when the bin has labels) and SVG outlines, and supports magnet holes and labels. TracetoForge runs its tracing in your browser, exports STL, 3MF, SVG and DXF, and has Packout and ToughSystem tray templates, but has no magnet holes or labels.

Is there a free photo to Gridfinity generator?

Yes, all four have a free option. TracetoForge: tracing and 3D preview are free, plus 3 export credits at signup. Tooltrace: 3 active traces free. GridPilot: 100 square inches a month free. Tracefinity: a small hosted free tier, or free and unlimited if you self-host.

Which tools export DXF for laser cutting or CNC?

TracetoForge, Tooltrace and GridPilot all list DXF export. Tracefinity exports SVG outlines but not DXF.

Which is most accurate?

None of these tools publish measured accuracy figures, so there is no fair winner to declare. In every one of them, scale comes from the paper in the photo, so a flat, evenly lit, top-down photo matters more than which tool you pick.

Do I need CAD skills to make a custom Gridfinity bin?

No. Photo-based generators exist to skip CAD. You photograph the tool on a sheet of paper, the software traces the outline and uses the paper for scale, and you export a printable file.

Can I sell what I export?

With TracetoForge, yes: you own the files you generate. For the other tools, check their terms before selling.
