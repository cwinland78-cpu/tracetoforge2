## Outline

### Quick Answer (top, callout)
> **Quick answer:** To install Gridfinity in a Milwaukee Packout drawer, print a 9×6 Gridfinity baseplate sized to fit the drawer interior (~390×245mm for the 48-22-8443 3-Drawer model), drop it in, and add Gridfinity bins designed for your tools. For the 48-22-8442 2-Drawer (~390×245×100mm internal), use the same baseplate footprint with deeper bins. Custom tool-shaped bins traced from photos work better than generic Gridfinity bins for hand tools. Total print time: ~6-8 hours per drawer; total cost: ~$8-12 in PETG.

### Why This Combination Works
Packout offers transport and stackability; Gridfinity offers in-drawer modularity. Together they solve "tools rattle in transit + can't find what I need at the jobsite."

### Step 1: Measure Your Drawer
Don't trust manufacturer specs alone — Milwaukee tolerances vary. Calipers, two diagonals, photograph for reference.

### Step 2: Print the Baseplate
- 9×6 Gridfinity baseplate (54 grid cells)
- Recommended baseplate type: lightweight (saves filament; you don't need magnets here)
- PETG, 0.2mm layer, 15% infill, 3 walls, no supports
- Total: ~6-8 hours, ~$5-8 filament

### Step 3: Choose Bin Style
Two paths:
- **Generic Gridfinity bins** (faster, less custom): pick from Printables/MakerWorld
- **Custom tool-shaped bins** (TracetoForge advantage): photograph your tools, trace, export, print

For hand tools that lie flat (pliers, wrenches, screwdrivers), custom tool-shaped wins. For loose hardware, generic bins win.

### Step 4: Print the Bins
Sizing notes:
- 1×1 cells (42mm) for small parts
- 2×1 / 2×2 for hand tools
- 3×1 / 3×2 for longer tools (wrenches up to ~250mm fit in 6×1 with overhang)
- Stacking lip: keep on (you may stack identical setups across drawers)

### Step 5: Drop In and Test
The baseplate sits flat in the drawer; bins drop into the baseplate. No glue, no screws — the baseplate weight + drawer wall friction holds it in place during transport. Stress-test: closed drawer, full-speed wagon over rough ground.

### Anti-Slip Recommendations
For the 2-Drawer (taller bins) at high transport speeds, add 6mm rubber feet to the baseplate underside if shifting occurs. (Mention: the Packout drawer has slight rubber-friction lining that usually suffices.)

### Print Time + Cost Breakdown for a Typical Setup
Worked example: 1 baseplate + 8 bins for a generic hand tool drawer.
- Baseplate: 6-8 hours, ~$5-8
- Bins: ~1 hour each × 8 = 8 hours total, ~$3-6
- Total: ~$8-14 + 14-16 hours print time

### FAQ
- Will a Gridfinity baseplate fit the 48-22-8424 Toolbox? *(no — the toolbox is much larger; you'd want a 12×8 or use a custom tray)*
- Can I use a magnetic baseplate? *(yes, but unnecessary for in-drawer use; magnets are for benchtop/wall mounting)*
- How do I stop bins from popping out when the drawer slams shut? *(weight + friction usually enough; if not, add the optional Gridfinity stacking lip clip)*
- Can I mix Gridfinity bins with non-Gridfinity inserts in the same drawer? *(yes — split the drawer with a baseplate covering, say, 6×6 of the 9×6, leaving the rest for a custom tray)*
